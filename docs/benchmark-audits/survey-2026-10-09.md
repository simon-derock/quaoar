**Written before any of these scans was run.** The sample, the order and the method below were fixed first, and every result is reported whatever it shows.

## Method
- **Same rules.** Ruleset version 1, thresholds unchanged (the 100× vendor-capital screen, the 18-month balance-sheet limit and the rest). Evidence is read as of each prospectus date.
- **Mode.** `--mode fixed` with `--max-credits 18` per scan, because about 97 SerpApi credits remained. The controls in the earlier audit ran in agent mode. The investigator only gathers evidence and never changes a verdict, so fixed mode can leave more lines at "couldn't find" but shouldn't create flags.
- **Order and stopping.** Scans run in the table's order. If the credits run out, the remaining prospectuses are listed as not scanned.
- **What counts as flagged.** Any line that doesn't match. Each flag is then checked by hand against its source, and the reading says whether it is about the company, its vendor or its banker.
- **No ground truth.** None of these companies has a known problem, so this measures how often Quaoar flags ordinary companies, not accuracy.

### The one flag, checked by hand
Scan 1's litigation line cites two Delhi court records naming **"Unitec Fibres Pvt. Ltd."**: a High Court order of 19 March 2025 and a commercial-court order of 27 August 2024, a commercial dispute with Green India Polytex. The prospectus discloses five matters but not these. The records are real. The name differs from the issuer only by "Pvt." and "Limited", so it is probably the same company, but that is not confirmed. A prospectus only has to list litigation above its materiality thresholds, so omitting a commercial dispute may be legitimate. The card words it as "worth a closer look", which is what it is.

## What went wrong, and what was fixed
The survey exposed three gaps in Quaoar itself. All three were parsing problems, fixed with tests first, with **no threshold or rule changed**.

1. **Two prospectuses could not be read (scans 3 and 4).** TNA headed its contents list "INDEX", and Spectraa printed contents lines as "GENERAL INFORMATION 81" with no dotted leaders. The section locator found nothing, so only the news check ran. The card then said "1 of 1 check out", which read as a complete check. Fixed: the locator now reads INDEX lists, lines without dotted leaders and "Page N of M" page headers, keeps a part heading out of the next title, and bridges small wording differences ("&" vs "AND", "LITIGATIONS" vs "LITIGATION"). All six prospectuses now locate all seven sections, and the five earlier prospectuses locate exactly what they did before. A card where no section is located now says so in a note. TNA was re-run with the fixed parser and is reported above.
2. **A numeric cover date was not read (scan 5).** Vaibhav's cover says "Dated: 07.10.2026". The scan fell back to "evidence as of today", which weakens the point-in-time guarantee for that card. The date reader now accepts numeric dates after "Dated". There were not enough credits left to re-run it, so the scan 5 card above was produced without a cutoff date.
3. **A bank was read as a vendor (scan 5).** The claim reader took "Sponsor Bank" for a vendor. It produced only "couldn't find" lines, never a flag, but it is an extraction error and is not fixed.

**Date correction.** The sample table originally listed Pind Hospitality as dated 19 September 2026, taken from a search listing. Its cover says 3 September 2025, and the scan used that date.

## Reading
- Counting the earlier audit's three controls (one flagged, over a real 2021 SEBI order about the lead manager), **2 of 7 ordinary companies had a line that didn't match.** Both are real records. Neither is an identity error of the kind the first control run exposed.
- This is a sample of seven, with no ground truth. It shows how often Quaoar flags ordinary companies. It does not measure accuracy.
- The survey's largest effect was on the tool: the unread-prospectus gap would otherwise have shipped.
